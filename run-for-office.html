CIVIC LEADERSHIP

Thinking about
running for office?

You don't have to begin with a national campaign. Learn the basics, understand your local rules, participate in your community, and decide whether public service is right for you.

01

Get involved

Attend a public meeting. Learn who represents you. Listen to the people around you.

02

Learn

Study your Constitution, local government, election rules, and the issues affecting your community.

03

Build

Build relationships and a record of constructive community participation.

04

Lead

Take responsibility for a project, issue, organization, or community effort.

05

Run

When you're ready, research eligibility, ballot access, filing requirements, and campaign-finance rules.

Federal candidate basics

The FEC says an individual generally becomes a federal candidate when contributions or expenditures exceed $5,000. Federal candidates then have registration and reporting requirements. State and local requirements differ.

Your first 30 days

  1. Identify your local, state, and federal representatives.
  2. Attend one public meeting.
  3. Read the Constitution and learn how your local government is structured.
  4. Talk with people in your community about local concerns.
  5. Research the official election authority for your state.

Official resources

Election requirements vary by jurisdiction. Use official government sources rather than relying on outdated summaries.
